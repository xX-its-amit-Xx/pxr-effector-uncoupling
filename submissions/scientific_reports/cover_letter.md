Dear Editor,

I am submitting the manuscript "Cell-type-resolved decoupling of PXR target genes identifies hepatocyte-selective readouts for xenobiotic response" for consideration at Scientific Reports.

This work delivers, to my knowledge, the first cell-type-resolved transcriptional coupling map of the pregnane X receptor (PXR; NR1I2) across 446,672 single human cells from ten Cell Ontology classes spanning four tissue compartments (liver, intestine, immune, placenta). The headline finding is biologically actionable: canonical PXR target coupling is selective for epithelial-barrier tissues — strongest in hepatocytes — and is essentially absent in circulating immune cells where the receptor is transcribed but transcriptionally inert. Six canonical targets show coupling Spearman rho 0.77 to 0.89 in hepatocytes (BH q approximately 0.004), with immune cells showing 4 to 8 fold smaller effect sizes despite formal statistical significance at our sample size of 50 to 110 k cells per immune type. The cell-type-resolved framing is what makes the result useful: it provides a small, actionable hepatocyte-selective biomarker panel (CYP2C8, CYP2C9, CYP3A5, ABCC2 — confirmed by direct rifamycin perturbation of primary human hepatocytes from Dyavar et al. 2020 *Sci Rep* 10:12565) for drug developers building next-generation tissue-restricted PXR modulators. PXR-driven drug-drug interactions cost lives in the clinic and complicate every drug-development program that touches CYP3A; identifying which gene readouts are direct PXR pharmacodynamics in hepatocytes versus shared-hepatic-TF couplings is the kind of "useful, broadly relevant, and methodologically rigorous" contribution that Scientific Reports exists to publish.

I believe this work is a strong fit for Scientific Reports for three reasons:

**Broad cross-disciplinary interest.** The finding intersects pharmacology (DDI biomarker discovery), single-cell biology (metacell methodology applied at atlas scale), and reproducible bioinformatics. Readers from any of those communities have a direct entry point.

**Technically rigorous and conservatively interpreted.** The headline is supported across six orthogonal validation layers: (i) parameter sweep across 17 combinations (median Spearman rho of decoupling rankings = 0.95) plus 20-round 80% cell-level subsampling; (ii) a matched 20-gene negative-control set including hepatocyte master TFs (Mann-Whitney U one-sided p = 1.0 x 10^-31; HNF4A and HNF1A do not show high decoupling, ruling out the generic-hepatocyte explanation); (iii) GTEx v8 bulk RNA-seq across 54 tissues (delta of +0.21 to +0.56 for all five top genes); (iv) direct rifamycin perturbation of primary human hepatocytes confirming four of the six top genes as PXR-responsive with up to 19-fold induction; (v) LINCS L1000 rifampicin response across 18 cell lines (HEPG2 ranks first); and (vi) the Open Targets v4 disease graph (top genes recover warfarin, statins, tacrolimus, cholestasis). The honest refinement that SLCO1B1 and CPT1A are coupled by shared hepatic-TF regulation rather than direct PXR control — surfaced by the perturbation overlay — is a defensible piece of careful interpretation that improves the actionable biomarker panel without weakening the cell-type-selectivity headline.

**Fully reproducible and openly available.** The complete pipeline is at https://github.com/xX-its-amit-Xx/pxr-effector-uncoupling under the MIT license, with locked Python 3.12 dependencies (uv.lock), 16 passing pytest unit tests, GitHub Actions CI, and a CITATION.cff. A Zenodo DOI will be minted upon acceptance. This is a single-author submission with no external funding; the reproducibility bundle is the substitute for institutional weight.

The work is original, not under consideration elsewhere, and has not been previously published. All figures are original to this work and were generated by the open-source pipeline; all data sources are publicly accessible and properly cited.

I look forward to the editor's response and to peer review.
